# Visual Geometry Reasoning

Geometry is unusual among theorem proving domains because the human mode of understanding is rarely purely symbolic. A person solving an olympiad problem reads the statement, sketches a diagram, notices alignments or symmetries, imagines additional points, and only then settles into explicit proof steps. GenesisGeo is motivated by the gap between that workflow and traditional text-only formal reasoning pipelines.

## Why text alone is insufficient

A purely textual theorem prover can manipulate statements accurately, but it may miss the structural cues that make a proof discoverable. In geometry, those cues often include:

- likely auxiliary points suggested by symmetry
- circles implied by angle equalities or equal distances
- parallel or perpendicular relationships that are diagrammatically salient
- special configurations such as incenter, orthocenter, or midpoint constructions

Many olympiad problems are not difficult because the final proof rule is obscure — they are difficult because the right intermediate construction is hard to guess.

## What multimodality means here

Multimodality here is not only about attaching an image encoder to a language model. It is about using visual information to constrain and prioritize reasoning:

- interpreting a geometric diagram as evidence about the likely configuration
- training on image-text-formal triples instead of text alone
- treating diagram understanding as an aid to proposing useful auxiliary constructions
- letting symbolic verification remain the final authority over correctness

Visual signals can suggest, but they do not certify. The symbolic engine remains responsible for proof validity.

## Why geometry is a good testbed

Geometry demands all of the following at once:

- compositional formal language
- long-horizon search
- latent intermediate objects
- grounded spatial intuition
- exact proof checking

That combination is rarer in mainstream theorem proving benchmarks, making geometry a compact environment for studying how learned models and symbolic systems can cooperate.
